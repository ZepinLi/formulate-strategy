# Smart Business Strategy

Read this reference for platform, ecosystem, network, data, AI, C2B, or digitally mediated business questions. It adapts Zeng Ming's strategic concepts without assuming that every business should become a platform or that AI automatically produces advantage.

## Classify the strategic system first

Identify the dominant operating logic before selecting the lens:

- **Linear business:** Value moves mainly through a controlled value chain. Scale, positioning, activity fit, process advantage, and bargaining structure may matter more than network language.
- **Networked business:** Independent participants create value through digitally enabled interaction, and the quality or range of participation affects other participants.
- **Generative or intelligent business:** Usage data and models continuously influence decisions, matching, creation, or service, potentially improving future interactions.

Hybrid models are common. State which logic dominates each critical activity. Do not relabel suppliers or customers as an ecosystem unless the relationships, incentives, and coordination mechanisms justify it.

## Focus discipline

Choose one primary ecosystem position, coordination bottleneck, or network/data feedback loop as the Strategic Focus. Linear, networked, and generative logics diagnose the system; `点线面体`, AI, platform, data intelligence, and C2B are lenses or mechanisms, not coequal priorities. The focus must state which resources shift toward the chosen position or loop and which adjacent opportunities are not being pursued.

## Network coordination — 网络协同

Map the system rather than merely naming participants:

- Who are the distinct actors, and what does each contribute, receive, and risk?
- Which interactions create the core value?
- Which transaction, search, trust, or coordination costs are reduced?
- What incentives encourage high-quality participation and discourage exploitation?
- Which side must arrive first, and how is the cold-start problem overcome?
- Does greater participation increase value, congestion, noise, or dependency?
- Who sets rules, resolves disputes, controls access, and bears externalities?
- Where is value created, and where can it be captured without weakening participation?

Require a causal account of network effects. A large user count alone is not a network effect.

## Data intelligence — 数据智能

Trace the complete feedback loop:

1. A real interaction produces relevant data with legitimate rights and consent.
2. The data improves a prediction, decision, match, generation, or workflow.
3. The improved output creates measurable user or ecosystem value.
4. Greater value produces more or better interactions.
5. The loop improves faster than cost, noise, risk, or model degradation grows.

Ask what decision becomes better, how quickly feedback arrives, whether the signal is attributable, and whether the improvement is proprietary or available to every competitor. Reject “more data” and “AI-powered” as strategies without this mechanism.

## Point–line–plane–body — 点线面体

Use this map only when it clarifies ecosystem position:

- **Point / 点:** A specialized service provider or participant operating in a larger network.
- **Line / 线:** A business, brand, or operator that integrates capabilities to serve a customer need.
- **Plane / 面:** A platform that enables and governs interactions among many participants.
- **Body / 体:** A broader ecosystem formed by interacting platforms, networks, and complementary roles.

Determine the current and desired position, the contribution required to earn that position, dependencies on adjacent layers, and the value-capture logic. Do not assume moving from point to line or line to plane is inherently superior; specialization may offer better economics and defensibility.

## Ecosystem structure test

Before accepting an ecosystem focus, map the interdependent activities required for one focal value proposition:

- The focal value proposition that cannot be delivered by one actor alone
- The actors required to realize it
- The activities each actor must perform
- The positions, rules, and flows connecting those activities
- The alignment risks that could prevent the value proposition from materializing

If this structure cannot be specified, use a more accurate construct such as a value chain, supplier system, distribution network, marketplace, or ordinary partnership. Ecosystem affiliation alone does not establish ecosystem strategy.

## C2B and intent-driven supply

For C2B or personalized systems, test whether customer intent can actually shape supply, design, allocation, or fulfillment. Distinguish among:

- Segment-level prediction
- Individual personalization
- Real-time matching or generation
- Customer intent that reorganizes upstream activity

Examine latency, minimum efficient scale, operational flexibility, data quality, willingness to share information, and the cost of serving heterogeneous demand.

## Strategy as generation

Under high uncertainty, a strategic direction can be stable while the path emerges through action and feedback. Use this idea to identify decisive assumptions and the smallest informative moves during formulation. Do not turn it into a recurring review cadence, automated iteration system, or justification for having no choices.

## Risk and governance gate

Any recommendation involving platforms, data, or AI must examine:

- Privacy, consent, data provenance, access, and deletion rights
- Bias, safety, security, explainability, and accountability where material
- Participant incentives, labor or supplier dependency, and asymmetric bargaining power
- Lock-in, single-platform dependency, and ecosystem fragility
- Cold-start subsidies and whether unit economics survive their removal
- Competition law, platform governance, and conflicts between operator and participants
- Whether model or data advantages are durable after competitors gain similar access

## Combined nonlinear and smart-business lens

For an AI or platform startup, combine the lenses in one causal thesis with one Strategic Focus:

1. An urgent initial problem and narrow beachhead
2. An order-of-magnitude improvement that users can recognize
3. A credible acquisition or distribution mechanism
4. A network or data feedback loop that strengthens with use
5. A lawful value-capture mechanism that preserves participant incentives
6. A route from the beachhead to a larger arena
7. A survival floor and evidence-based commitment sequence

If any link is missing, identify it as an assumption rather than filling the gap with platform or AI rhetoric.
