# Founder Growth

Read this reference when the user is considering a startup, developing as a founder, evaluating an idea, finding early users, diagnosing growth, deciding how closely to operate, or protecting runway. A startup is one first-class path within personal growth, not the default destination for ambitious people.

Integrate this lens into one Current Growth Focus. Do not produce a separate company-strategy report, platform framework, organization design, fundraising playbook, or growth-at-any-cost plan.

## Route by founder stage

Identify the stage before giving advice:

1. **Before a startup:** Determine whether the user has a problem worth inhabiting, relevant ability or access, an owned project that can test it, and an affordable way to learn without prematurely quitting or fundraising.
2. **Idea discovery:** Look for problems the founder has experienced or understands unusually well, people the founder can reach, and a small group that needs a solution urgently.
3. **Early startup:** Seek direct user contact, manual recruitment, exceptional service, rapid product learning, and evidence that the initial users care.
4. **Emerging traction:** Distinguish retained value from noisy acquisition; identify the actual growth loop, economics, capacity constraint, and default-alive trajectory.
5. **Scaling founder:** Preserve responsibility and access to ground truth while making explicit which decisions require founder depth and which should be delegated. Do not use founder involvement as a reason to micromanage.

When stages are mixed, diagnose the earliest unresolved uncertainty. A startup without evidence of urgent value is not ready for a scaling strategy.

## Founder–problem signal

Prefer ideas that emerge from contact with reality:

- The founder or reachable users experience the problem directly.
- The founder can build, deliver, or orchestrate a first useful version.
- A narrow group needs the result intensely rather than a broad group liking it abstractly.
- The founder has an edge in insight, access, taste, speed, credibility, or technical ability.
- There is a plausible path from the initial niche to a larger opportunity if the narrow thesis proves true.

Do not manufacture startup ideas by combining fashionable technologies and large markets. An idea can be non-obvious without being contrarian theater. Treat founder intuition as a lead to test, not market evidence.

## One startup thesis

When a startup is the selected Growth Focus, make the thesis concrete:

> For `[initial user]` who struggles with `[urgent problem]`, build `[owned product or service]` that creates `[valuable outcome]`; learn through `[direct early-user loop]`, compound through `[one growth or ownership mechanism]`, and increase commitment only when `[decisive evidence]` appears while protecting `[runway or survival floor]`.

Require one primary initial user, problem, product thesis, learning loop, and compounding mechanism. Adjacent markets, platform ambitions, and feature portfolios remain later possibilities, not coequal focuses.

## Do the unscalable work that produces truth

Early effort should reduce uncertainty or create exceptional value:

- Recruit users manually when this reveals who cares and why.
- Observe real workflows and do parts of the service by hand.
- Give early users unusually close attention and notice repeated friction.
- Build the smallest complete experience that can produce a strong response.
- Record what should eventually become product, distribution, or operating leverage.

Unscalable work is not a virtue by itself. Stop doing it when it neither improves user value nor reveals a repeatable engine. Avoid launch fantasies, premature partnerships, and automation before understanding the work.

## Growth quality

Use growth as feedback only after defining what healthy growth means. Examine:

- Whether target users retain, return, pay, refer, or deepen usage
- Whether the product creates the promised user outcome
- Which acquisition or usage loop causes growth and whether it strengthens with use
- Whether contribution economics and operating capacity improve or deteriorate
- Whether growth depends on subsidies, founder heroics, or a channel the startup does not control
- Whether the company is a startup designed for scalable growth or a valuable small business with different economics and ambitions

Do not infer product value from sign-ups, publicity, fundraising, or gross usage alone. Name the weakest link in the growth loop.

## Founder growth and involvement

Treat resourcefulness as the ability to find a route through obstacles while staying in contact with users and facts. Be stubborn about the high-level valuable outcome and flexible about product, channel, role, and method.

Protect the founder's top idea: identify which issue repeatedly occupies background attention and whether fundraising, status contests, disputes, or operational noise is displacing product and user truth.

Use **Founder Mode** only as a hypothesis that responsibility and information proximity may require nonstandard involvement. It is not permission to bypass capable leaders, centralize every decision, or manage through fear. Specify:

- Which high-leverage decisions need founder judgment
- Which ground-truth channels the founder must keep direct
- Which responsibilities have clear owners and can be delegated
- Which intervention would create dependency or destroy accountability

The boundary should change with evidence, team capability, and stage.

## Survival before irreversible commitment

For any decision to quit, hire, raise, scale, or extend runway, make the survival logic explicit:

1. **Default alive or dead:** On a realistic revenue, cost, and cash trajectory, does the startup reach sustainability before cash runs out?
2. **Affordable loss:** What bounded time, money, reputation, health, or opportunity cost can the founder lose and still retain future choices?
3. **Runway:** Which assumptions determine the time available, and what happens under a less favorable case?
4. **Plan B:** What reversible validation can precede an irreversible commitment?
5. **Premature scale:** Would hiring, fundraising, or infrastructure amplify truth—or conceal the absence of it?
6. **Personal floor:** Which health, relationship, legal, ethical, or financial constraints cannot be traded away?

Never equate courage with all-in exposure. Concentrated attention can coexist with staged capital and protected downside.

## Persist, pivot, and stop

State conditions in observable terms:

- **Persist** when the same target users repeatedly demonstrate strong value and the learning or growth loop improves.
- **Pivot** when the founder-problem edge remains valuable but the user, problem framing, product, or channel assumption fails.
- **Stop** when urgent demand does not appear after adequate direct testing, the economics or risks violate the survival floor, or a superior growth focus now has materially stronger evidence.

Do not set arbitrary dates or vanity thresholds. Match the evidence window to the product's natural usage and buying cycle.

## Primary Paul Graham sources

- [How to Get Startup Ideas](https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html): lived problems, urgent narrow users, founder ability, and paths out of a niche.
- [Do Things that Don't Scale](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html): manual recruitment, close service, and early learning.
- [Startup = Growth](https://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html): the designed-for-growth distinction and growth as a compass.
- [Default Alive or Default Dead?](https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html): runway awareness, profitability trajectory, and early action.
- [The Right Kind of Stubborn](https://www.paulgraham.com/persistence.html): persistence at the goal level with tactical flexibility.
- [Founder Mode](https://www.paulgraham.com/foundermode.html): a provocative account of founder involvement that requires the governance safeguards above.
