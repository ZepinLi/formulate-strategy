# Relationships, Conditions, and Strategic Action

Use the relevant sections when a diagnosis must explain changing relationships, a practical entry differs from the governing constraint, or coordinated resources and subsequent conditions matter. This reference develops the main six-step reasoning; it is not a second workflow or a required philosophy lecture.

## Explain relationships and their changing conditions

An inventory of personal attributes or resources is not yet an explanation. Identify which relationships make the observed situation possible: a skill matters in relation to a problem; a tool's value depends on its workflow; collaborators' contributions depend on access, commitments, and complementary abilities. Bound the process rather than mapping every possible influence.

Ask how the activity that sustains a relationship can alter its conditions. For example, close manual service may create user value and useful insight, then attract enough users to exhaust the same person's capacity. More rules may improve familiar cases while their interactions undermine reliability elsewhere. These are hypotheses about particular mechanisms, not a rule that every success must destroy itself.

Look for evidence of what is being strengthened, depleted, made dependent, or displaced. Include external changes; an internal explanation is not automatically superior. An explanation should make a difference to what one expects or does. If the same recommendation follows regardless of the asserted mechanism, clarify what the diagnosis contributes.

Distinguish a material tension from a logical inconsistency. If using the term **contradiction (矛盾)**, identify the actual relationship and how its tendencies depend on and constrain each other. Listing advantages and disadvantages, naming a difficulty, or treating a proposition and its negation as true in the same sense does not establish such a mechanism.

## Distinguish diagnosis, stage task, and entry

The source concepts and practical decisions are related but not interchangeable:

| Concept | Question it addresses | Guard against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principal contradiction (主要矛盾) | Within the source's account, which relationship governs the development of other contradictions at this stage? | Calling the most urgent, difficult, or actionable item the principal contradiction without showing its wider effect |
| Principal aspect (矛盾的主要方面) | Within a specified contradiction, which side currently has the governing position? | Confusing a side of a relationship with a different problem or a whole system's priority |
| Stage task (阶段任务) | What should change or be protected during this phase? | Mistaking an explanatory diagnosis for an action instruction |
| Practical entry (突破口) | Where can action now advance that task or discriminate its assumptions? | Choosing an easy local win with no link to the wider objective |

“Whole/local” concerns scope; “principal/secondary” concerns relative role. Neither pair maps automatically onto the other. The person may be unable to change a governing condition directly. An entry can instead produce evidence, access, capability, or cooperation that makes a later change possible.

Test a candidate diagnosis by tracing its influence on other constraints and comparing an alternative explanation. Consider what would remain blocked if it changed alone. Coupled constraints may require a coordinated intervention or a sequence of enabling actions. Mao's text asserts a single principal contradiction; this skill deliberately treats dominance as a testable judgment rather than requiring every modern problem to have one unique bottleneck.

## Connect local action to the wider process

Choose an entry jointly for feasibility now, contribution to the stage task, and its effect on what can follow. A small action is useful because of its consequence or information, not simply because it is easy. A prestigious or difficult action is not necessarily consequential either.

For a real choice, compare a few entries and ask:

- Which assumptions and resources make this attempt feasible?
- What important condition would the result change or preserve?
- What remains blocked afterwards, and can the person realistically act on the result?
- How might relevant users, collaborators, or other actors respond? Where are interests shared or different?
- After failure, which resources, choices, or commitments remain?

Think through at least the next phase without pretending to predict the whole path. A useful option may be a test, preparation, a smaller complete result, or waiting for a specific prerequisite. Waiting needs a condition for reconsideration and an account of the cost of delay; do not assume an opportunity will inevitably appear.

Do not transfer the military requirement of confidence in a first battle into a requirement that research must guarantee a positive finding. For an affordable experiment, sufficient support concerns the value of the question, the method, access, and bounded exposure. Whether it actually discriminated the hypothesis must be checked afterwards. For a larger irreversible commitment, stronger evidence and a more robust fallback are needed. See [decision-calibration.md](decision-calibration.md) for the burden of proof and early-work judgments.

## Concentrate with support and affordable losses

Treat resources as a feasible combination, not a total on a balance sheet. Time without the required skill, data without permission, or a willing collaborator without usable inputs may not enable the result. Distinguish resources actually available from hoped-for access or assistance, and state any material unconfirmed commitment.

Organize main effort, complementary work, essential maintenance, and retained capacity around the stage task. A single focus can contain several coordinated activities. Bounded exploration can contain sequential projects. Concentration does not require cancelling everything else, and sufficient resources are not defined by a universal percentage or a historical troop ratio.

Name what is deferred and what loss that incurs. Check whether a supposedly temporary loss is actually reversible: lost trust, a closed opportunity window, or time with family may not be recoverable. Future success is not proof that the cost will be repaid. Protect the survival floor, obligations, and the user's own values.

When the resource combination cannot support the desired result, change its scope, acquire a prerequisite, alter timing, or select another entry. Do not substitute urgency, courage, or “focus harder” for missing conditions. Founder-specific user dependence and runway checks remain in [founder-growth.md](founder-growth.md), not a general organizational-management module here.

## Turn results into subsequent conditions

Evaluate what changed after costs: a checked finding, useful tool, reusable method, trusted collaboration, clearer user knowledge, or preserved capacity. Increased output, reach, or activity can coexist with a worse position if maintenance, dependencies, or loss of options grow faster.

Explain how a useful result is retained and used. Avoid claiming accumulated capability from an artifact that cannot be inspected, reused, transferred, or used to revise the next action. Conversely, a valuable one-time result can justify the effort without a perpetual compounding story. The distinctions between compounding, thresholds, and their combination stay in [decision-calibration.md](decision-calibration.md).

After a result, inspect both what it demonstrates and what it has changed. A practice may have been effective before success increased its load, changed the role, or altered others' responses. Separate that case from an explanation that was wrong from the beginning, failure to implement a critical requirement, and still-unclear external causes. More than one may be involved.

Revise the limiting form while retaining what the evidence supports. In practical terms, say what to keep, what to remove or change, and how to test the change. This is a methodological analogy to determinate negation and preservation, not a claim that each project follows Hegel's logic or that every change is progress. Evidence may justify preserving an existing condition or ending the project rather than a larger next stage.

## Worked research example

This is an invented illustration, not a historical author's technical analysis.

1. **Situation:** A researcher seeks generalizable compiler optimizations. Added special cases improve a fixed benchmark, while unseen programs regress and rule interactions raise maintenance cost. The same method generates both the visible gains and a growing limit.
2. **Judgment:** The hypothesis is that gains depend on case-specific rules whose interactions are not controlled. A competing explanation is that measurements or workload selection are misleading. The stage task is to establish transferable, checkable evidence, not simply a higher benchmark score.
3. **Entry:** Compare adding more cases with isolating one transformation and its applicability boundary. Choose the latter if feasible: test it against independent workloads and counterexamples, preserving useful old results as a comparison. It can reveal whether a reusable mechanism exists without proving it in advance.
4. **Deployment:** Concentrate the available research time on that transformation, measurements, and correctness checks; retain necessary maintenance and use a collaborator's review only where agreed. Narrow the workload if the capacity is insufficient rather than promising a complete optimizer.
5. **Result:** A verified improvement with limits, a counterexample, or a reproducible negative finding changes what can reasonably be tried next. Count implementation and maintenance costs; do not relabel every unsuccessful run as knowledge.
6. **Update:** If the independent measurement invalidates the original gain, revise the diagnosis. If a sound method later becomes difficult to compose as rules grow, reassess the changed conditions. Preserve valid transformations while testing a different composition method; neither a full rewrite nor continued patching follows automatically.

## Worked founder example

This is an invented illustration at the founder's own resource-allocation level.

1. **Situation:** A founder's direct manual service produces user value and insight, attracting more users. Repeated interruptions now consume the founder's limited capacity and delay reliable delivery.
2. **Judgment:** Examine support records before asserting the cause. Repeated common needs, custom exceptions, and product defects call for different responses. The stage task is to preserve validated user value while reducing dependence on the founder's repeated manual work.
3. **Entry:** Compare taking more users, stopping all close service, and testing a complete common workflow with a small suitable cohort. Select the third when common needs and delivery conditions support it, and consider how users might react to less customization.
4. **Deployment:** Assign most discretionary effort to the common workflow while preserving critical support, direct contact, and the founder's income and rest boundaries. Defer new segments and unnecessary exceptions. Extra users or hires do not themselves solve an untested product or service model.
5. **Result:** Inspect whether the cohort still achieves its outcome and whether total support burden falls. Reusable product behavior and explicit service boundaries may enable another bounded cohort; losing user value would undermine that inference.
6. **Update:** The earlier close service need not have been wrong: its success may have changed the capacity conditions. Keep the useful contact and learned needs, change the source of dependence, and adjust or stop expansion if the proposed change does not hold. Do not automatically convert this into a company governance redesign.

## Source locations, context, and transfer limits

The six-step reasoning and examples are this skill's synthesis. The user-supplied discussions generated questions; the primary texts below support the mappings. Historical claims, explanatory hypotheses, and modern instructions are distinct.

| Source and location | Original context | Instruction informed here | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Hegel, *Encyclopaedia Logic*, addition to §81](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/sl/sl_divis.htm); [SEP clarification](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/) | A philosophical account of finite determinations and their own limits; SEP explains cancelling and preserving | Ask whether a form's activity produces its limits; specify what remains valid when changing it | Not a universal thesis–antithesis–compromise recipe or an empirical guarantee of necessary progress |
| [Marx, 1859 Preface](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/preface.htm), relations becoming fetters; [*Theses on Feuerbach*, III](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1845/theses/theses.htm) | Historical production relations; practice changes circumstances and people | Examine enabling relations historically and how action changes both conditions and capability | Contemporary research and founder cases are analogies, not Marx's direct analyses or a complete theory of his work |
| [Mao, *On Contradiction* (1937), III–V](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-193708.htm) | Particularity, principal contradiction, principal aspect, and conditional transformation | Specify process and stage, distinguish the concepts, and investigate relative influence | The source's universal single-principal claim is not imposed as a modern single-cause requirement |
| [*Problems of Strategy in China's Revolutionary War* (1936), I.3 and V.5–6, V.9](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-193612.htm) | Revolutionary war, overall weakness and selected local action; the first engagement, deployment, and material consequences | Connect scope and stages; choose an entry by feasibility and subsequent effect; assess outcomes after consumption | No universal enemy, guaranteed scientific success, numerical allocation ratio, or claim that local victories guarantee the wider objective |
| [*Concentrate a Superior Force to Destroy the Enemy Forces One by One* (1946), II, VII–IX](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19460916.htm) | Conditional deployment, support forces, and circumstances permitting multiple attacks | Judge sufficiency against the task and coordinate main and supporting effort | Military force and destruction are not prescriptions for personal work or collaboration |
| [*Why Is It That Red Political Power Can Exist in China?* (1928), II, IV–VI](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19281005.htm); [*A Single Spark Can Start a Prairie Fire* (1930)](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19300105.htm), base-building and the closing discussion of timing | Specific political, economic, social, organizational, and military conditions | Examine what sustains small-scale action and how retained results support a further attempt | The source's necessity and timing claims are not promises that a project will grow. Later victory does not validate every earlier prediction |
| [*Some Questions Concerning Methods of Leadership* (1943), II, VI, VIII](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19430601.htm); [*Methods of Work of Party Committees* (1949), V–VII](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19490313.htm) | Concrete experience, a stage center with supporting work, coordination and actual quantities | Combine a priority with support, real inputs, and evidence from a limited attempt | Does not expand this skill into organizational doctrine or command over others |
| [*On Practice* (1937)](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-193707.htm), paragraphs on revision within a stage and transition to another | Knowing, acting, correcting mistakes, and following an objectively changing process | Distinguish a mistaken account from changed conditions; reassess after results | The four update possibilities in the main skill are an operational extension, not a quoted classification; one outcome cannot identify all causes |

The texts differ in date and intellectual purpose. Do not present Hegel, Marx, and Mao as one identical system or write the 1937 philosophical formulations back into a completed 1930 action manual. Keep original claims separate from this skill's additional requirements for counterexamples, competing explanations, and bounded loss. No philosophical label excuses a logical error or replaces concrete evidence.
