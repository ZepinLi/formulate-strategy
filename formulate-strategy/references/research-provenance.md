# Research Provenance

Compiled on 2026-09-05. Use this reference when explaining, auditing, or updating the skill's methodology. The operational instructions synthesize the sources; they do not treat any author or community skill as unquestionable authority.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Original works and first-party author publications
2. Peer-reviewed research and authoritative institutional material
3. Reputable publisher or practitioner summaries when primary text is unavailable
4. Community skills as interaction-design precedents only

For contested or context-dependent claims, triangulate across schools of thought and label inference. Do not infer methodological quality from popularity, installation count, or repository stars.

## Operational synthesis: one Strategic Focus

The mandatory focus sentence is an operational synthesis rather than a quotation or complete model from any one source. It combines:

- Rumelt's concentration of coordinated power on an important, addressable challenge
- Porter's scope, trade-offs, exclusions, and activity-system fit
- Martin's linked where-to-play and how-to-win choices
- Collis and Rukstad's objective, scope, and advantage statement
- Resource-allocation research showing that commitments, not declarations, reveal realized strategy

The resulting invariant is: one strategy at one decision scope has one current focus, expressed through a chosen target, objective and horizon, causal advantage mechanism, disproportionate resource allocation, and consequential exclusion. The strategy kernel, choice cascade, capabilities, activity system, and evidence exist to support or challenge that focus; they are not coequal frameworks.

## Core strategy sources

| Source | Contribution to this skill | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy/Bad Strategy* [publisher preview](https://profilebooks.com/wp-content/uploads/wpallimport/files/PDFs/9781847657466_preview.pdf), [The Crux discussion](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/getting-strategy-wrong-and-how-to-do-it-right-instead), and [McKinsey interview](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/why-bad-strategy-is-a-social-contagion) | Diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action, crux, and concentration of coordinated power on the selected challenge | Focus can be applied to the wrong target if diagnosis and external evidence are weak; does not by itself provide a complete option-generation process |
| Michael Porter, [*What Is Strategy?*](https://hbr.org/1996/11/what-is-strategy) | Positioning, trade-offs, activity fit, operational effectiveness distinction | Competitive-positioning language needs translation for personal strategy and fast-changing ecosystems |
| Roger Martin, [strategy overview](https://rogerlmartin.com/thought-pillars/strategy), [choice cascade](https://rogerlmartin.com/docs/default-source/articles/strategy/scs.pdf?sfvrsn=28f50182_0), and [*Bringing Science to the Art of Strategy*](https://hbr.org/2012/09/bringing-science-to-the-art-of-strategy) | Integrated choice cascade, multiple possibilities, “what must be true,” barrier testing | A cascade without diagnosis can become a fill-in-the-blank exercise |
| David Collis and Michael Rukstad, [*Can You Say What Your Strategy Is?*](https://hbr.org/2008/04/can-you-say-what-your-strategy-is) | A concise strategy statement combining objective, scope, and advantage | A concise statement can still become a slogan unless linked to the crux, resource concentration, coherent action, and evidence |
| Gary Hamel and C. K. Prahalad, [*Strategic Intent*](https://hbr.org/2005/07/strategic-intent) | A stable ambition that concentrates attention and encourages leverage beyond current resources | Intent is not sufficient strategy without scope, advantage, trade-offs, resource allocation, and a causal diagnosis |
| Jay Barney, [*Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/014920639101700108) | Tests whether resources underlying advantage are valuable, rare, difficult to imitate, and difficult to substitute | Resource attributes alone do not select the arena, diagnose the crux, or ensure that customers and stakeholders value the result |
| David Teece, Gary Pisano, and Amy Shuen, [*Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management*](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v18y1997i7p509-533.html) | Connects advantage under technological change to processes, asset positions, path dependence, and reconfiguration | Dynamic capability language can become unfalsifiable unless a specific capability, environmental change, and outcome mechanism are named |
| Henry Mintzberg, [*Strategies, Deliberate and Emergent*](https://mintzberg.org/articles/strategies-deliberate-and-emergent) | Balance between intent and learning from emerging patterns | Emergence does not remove the need to choose or commit |
| James March, [*Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71) | Exposes the allocation tension between refining current certainties and exploring new possibilities | Does not prescribe one universal allocation; exploration cannot become an excuse for multiple coequal focuses |
| Tomo Noda and Joseph Bower, [*Strategy Making as Iterated Processes of Resource Allocation*](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.4250171011) | Shows how strategic commitments emerge through repeated resource-allocation decisions | Evidence comes from large-firm process research and should not imply that every strategy needs corporate budgeting machinery |
| Catherine Maritan and Gwendolyn Lee, [*Resource Allocation and Strategy*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0149206317729738) | Establishes financial, physical, technological, and human allocation as central to realized strategy | The literature is thinner than the importance of the topic suggests; allocation direction may be more defensible than false numerical precision |
| Rita Gunther McGrath and Ian C. MacMillan, [*Discovery-Driven Planning*](https://hbr.org/1995/07/discovery-driven-planning) | Explicit assumptions and evidence-gated commitments under uncertainty | Validation mechanics should remain proportional to the decision |
| Saras D. Sarasvathy, [*Causation and Effectuation*](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.2001.4378020) | Control, available means, partnerships, contingencies, and affordable loss | Effectual reasoning is not a substitute for market or stakeholder evidence |
| Donald Sull and Kathleen Eisenhardt, [*Simple Rules for a Complex World*](https://hbr.org/2012/09/simple-rules-for-a-complex-world) | A small set of decision rules for repeated strategic choices | Rules should follow a strategy; they are not the strategy itself |
| Dan Lovallo and Daniel Kahneman, [*Delusions of Success*](https://hbr.org/2003/07/delusions-of-success-how-optimism-undermines-executives-decisions) | Outside-view and reference-class reasoning to counter optimism, anchoring, and competitor neglect | Base rates inform commitment but cannot decide values, create insight, or replace case-specific causal reasoning |

## Maintenance rule

When updating the methodology, record the new source, the claim it supports, its limitations, and the instruction it changes. Prefer replacing a weaker rule over accumulating overlapping frameworks. Keep personal automation, scheduled reviews, and integrations outside this skill unless the user explicitly expands its scope.
